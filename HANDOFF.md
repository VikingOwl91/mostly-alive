# Project Handoff — Mostly Alive Guide

## 0. Mission

Build an open-source, web-first safety knowledge base that teaches rare-but-important everyday survival knowledge through memorable, dry, absurdist humor.

The core idea:

> People remember funny things.
>
> Therefore: make important safety knowledge funny enough to remember, while keeping the actual instructions precise, sober, sourced, and unmistakable.

The project should feel like an overly confident electronic field guide written for humans by an entity that considers humans fascinating, fragile, and statistically questionable.

The tone may be inspired by absurdist sci-fi guidebooks, but the project **must have its own identity**.

Do **not** copy protected Hitchhiker's Guide branding, logos, character names, specific visual assets, distinctive passages, or trademarked presentation.

Create an original design language that captures:

- retro-futuristic electronic guide
- terminal / field-manual aesthetics
- dry understatement
- absurd categorisation
- unnecessarily formal warnings
- calm presentation of alarming information
- occasional footnotes that make the joke worse

Working title:

# Mostly Alive

Possible subtitle:

> A practical guide to remaining so.

Alternative names may be proposed before final branding is locked.

---

# 1. Product Goal

The site should answer questions like:

- Why are my hairs suddenly standing upright during a storm?
- What do I do if the sea suddenly retreats?
- Why should I never pour water onto burning oil?
- What do I do if a power line falls near me?
- What do I do if ice breaks beneath me?
- What does carbon monoxide exposure look like?
- What should I do if my car enters water?
- What if I smell gas?
- What if someone is choking?
- What if I encounter an aggressive animal?

These topics should be presented in a way that users actually remember.

The site is **not** intended to be:

- a prepper website
- a disaster fetish site
- a medical replacement
- an emergency services replacement
- a survivalist gear catalogue
- a fear-driven publication

It should instead feel like:

> Useful knowledge humanity probably should have mentioned earlier.

# 2. Core Content Pattern

Every safety article should separate **humor** from **actionable facts**.

Recommended structure:

```text
Title

Short humorous introduction

Threat / urgency metadata

Immediate Action
What to do right now

Do Not
Common dangerous mistakes

Why This Happens
Short explanation

What Happens Next
Follow-up actions

When To Call Emergency Services

Memory Hook
One extremely memorable sentence

Sources
Primary / authoritative references

Last Reviewed
Date + reviewer
```

Example:

# HAIR, SUDDENLY VERTICAL

**Category:** Atmospheric inconveniences
**Urgency:** Immediate
**Threat Level:** ⚡⚡⚡⚡⚡

Humans spend considerable amounts of money persuading their hair to achieve impressive volume.

Occasionally the atmosphere will provide this service entirely free of charge.\*

- There will, in fact, be considerable charge.

## Immediate Action

If your hair stands on end, your skin tingles, or you hear buzzing/crackling while outdoors during storm conditions:

- leave exposed terrain immediately
- move away from ridges, hilltops and isolated tall objects
- seek substantial shelter
- a fully enclosed vehicle is preferable to remaining exposed

Do not remain in place to record the phenomenon.

## Important

Hair standing upright is **not a reliable early-warning system**.

Lightning can strike without this sign.

# 8. Sources and Editorial Quality

This is a safety website.

Claims must not be based on random blogs.

Prefer primary or authoritative sources such as:

- government agencies
- national meteorological services
- fire departments
- emergency management agencies
- Red Cross / Red Crescent
- WHO
- national health services
- poison control organisations
- coast guards
- geological agencies
- transport authorities
- electrical safety authorities

Secondary sources may supplement primary sources.

Each article should expose its sources publicly.

# 25. Editorial Style Guide

Humor:

- dry
- concise
- understated
- observational
- occasionally absurd
- never mock victims
- never trivialise suffering
- never delay urgent instructions

Good:

> The electrical grid has several opinions about being touched. Most are negative.

Bad:

> Touch this and you'll die lol.

The narrator should feel intelligent, detached, slightly exasperated, but fundamentally helpful.

Humans spend considerable amounts of money persuading their hair to achieve impressive volume.

3. Humor must never obscure an instruction.

# 45. Final Product Test

Before considering the first version successful, ask:

> If somebody read one article about an obscure hazard two years ago and then unexpectedly encountered that hazard today, would they remember what to do?

If the answer is yes:

the guide is doing its job.

If they also remember the joke:

even better.

If they remember only the joke:

rewrite the article.
