Encountering a venomous snake while hiking, gardening, or camping can result in an instantaneous defensive strike.

Venomous snakes possess specialized hollow or grooved fangs designed to inject a complex biochemical cocktail of enzymes, metalloproteinases, neurotoxins, and cardiotoxins into subcutaneous tissue or muscle.

### The Physics of Envenomation: The Lymphatic Pump

Unlike small-molecule pharmaceutical drugs, snake venoms consist of **large, heavy protein macromolecules** (molecular weight typically 6,000 to 100,000 Daltons).

- **The Lymphatic Route**: Because these molecules are too large to pass through the walls of continuous blood capillaries, they enter the body through the **lymphatic system**.
- **The Muscular Pump**: Lymph fluid has no heart to pump it; it moves solely through the rhythmic contraction and relaxation of surrounding skeletal muscles.
- **The Implication**: If a bitten person panics, runs, or waves their arms, their skeletal muscles act as an active hydraulic pump, shooting venom straight into central circulation. **Total physical stillness and splinting of the limb slows venom distribution by more than 70%.**

### Hollywood Myths That Destroy Limbs

For decades, survival novels and western films popularized aggressive, harmful interventions that modern toxicology strictly condemns:

1. **The "Cut-and-Suck" Fallacy**: Slicing the fang punctures with a pocketknife and trying to suck out venom recovers less than **0.1% of injected venom**, while severing digital nerves, lacerating tendons, and introducing virulent oral bacteria (_e.g., Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus_) into damaged tissue.
2. **The Tourniquet Danger (Vipers / Pit Vipers)**: In bites from vipers, rattlesnakes, and adders, the venom is heavily _cytotoxic_ and _hemotoxic_ (destroying cells and blood vessels). Applying a tight tourniquet concentrates this cellular poison entirely in the lower extremity, guaranteeing massive compartment syndrome, tissue gangrene, and surgical amputation.
3. **The Ice Fallacy**: Applying ice packs causes severe microvascular vasoconstriction, freezing the already poisoned, hypoxic tissue and accelerating gangrene.

### The Modern Clinical Protocol

Keep the patient calm, sitting or lying down. **Immobilize the limb with a splint at heart level**. Remove all rings and watches immediately. Mark the swelling frontier with a pen and timestamp every 15 minutes, and proceed directly by emergency ambulance to a hospital stocked with specific antivenom.
