A high-energy industrial saw blade, agricultural machinery, severe crush injury, or vehicular collision can result in a traumatic amputation—the complete severance of a finger, hand, foot, or entire limb.

In traumatic amputation, two distinct medical missions occur simultaneously:

1. **The Primary Mission**: Preventing the patient from bleeding to death from severed major vessels within 180 seconds.
2. **The Secondary Mission**: Preserving the severed tissue in a viable state for microvascular replantation surgery within the warm/cold ischemia window.

### The Doctrine: Life Over Limb

The most common mistake by untrained bystanders is immediately panicking about finding and saving the severed limb while the patient silently exsanguinates onto the floor.

- **Arterial Hemorrhage**: Severed radial, brachial, or femoral arteries can pump out 500 mL of blood every 15 to 30 seconds.
- **Tourniquet Application**: If direct manual pressure with a bulky sterile dressing does not instantly halt bleeding, apply a commercial windlass tourniquet (e.g., CAT or SAM-XT) 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 inches) above the injury. Tighten until bleeding stops completely and record the time of application on the patient’s forehead.

### The Amputate Preservation Protocol: The 3-Layer System

To successfully reattach a severed body part, microvascular surgeons must sew together microscopic arteries and veins measuring under 1 to 2 millimeters in diameter under high-power surgical microscopes.

If the amputated part is mishandled, replantation becomes biologically impossible:

1. **Layer 1 (The Sterile Wrap)**: Wrap the severed part in clean, sterile, dry gauze (or very lightly moistened with saline). Never scrub the severed margins.
2. **Layer 2 (The Watertight Seal)**: Place the wrapped part inside a clean, waterproof plastic bag (such as a Ziploc bag) and seal it tightly.
3. **Layer 3 (The Ice-Water Slurry)**: Place the sealed bag into a second container filled with a mixture of **ice and cold water**.
   - The water acts as a thermal buffer, keeping the tissue at an optimal **+4°C (39°F)**.
   - **Why Raw Ice is Lethal**: Direct contact with ice or dry ice causes intracellular water to form sharp ice crystals that puncture cell membranes, causing full-thickness frostbite necrosis. A frozen digit cannot be reattached.
   - **Why Submerging in Water is Lethal**: Soaking the raw tissue directly in water causes hypotonic osmotic swelling, dissolving cellular architecture (_maceration_).
