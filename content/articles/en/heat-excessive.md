When ambient air temperature and relative humidity rise, the human body's primary cooling mechanism—evaporative sweat cooling—breaks down. If internal metabolic heat generation exceeds environmental dissipation, core body temperature surges past 40°C (104°F).

### The Cellular Destruction of Hyperthermia

At core temperatures above 40.5°C (105°F), cellular enzymes and structural proteins throughout the brain, liver, and cardiovascular system undergo thermal denaturation.

The blood-brain barrier breaks down, causing cerebral edema and neurological collapse (manifesting as agitation, bizarre behavior, seizures, and coma). Meanwhile, the intestinal mucosal barrier becomes permeable, dumping bacterial endotoxins directly into the circulation and triggering systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), and multi-organ failure.

### The "Cool First, Transport Second" Mandate

In exertional heat stroke, mortality is directly proportional to the total time spent above critical temperature thresholds. Waiting for an ambulance before beginning active cooling increases mortality drastically. Whole-body immersion in cold or iced water extracts heat at over **0.20°C per minute**, rapidly returning core temperature to safe levels and halting the systemic inflammatory cascade.
