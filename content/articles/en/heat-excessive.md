The human metabolic apparatus operates within a strict thermal window centered at approximately 37°C (98.6°F). When ambient heat and humidity overwhelm the body’s evaporative sweat mechanisms, core temperature climbs past 40°C (104°F).

At this threshold, enzymes denature, the blood-brain barrier becomes permeable, and systemic cellular inflammation begins.

### The Diagnostic Triage: Exhaustion vs. Stroke

| Indicator            | Heat Exhaustion                        | Heat Stroke (Acute Emergency)                                   |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mental Status**    | Alert, coherent, fatigued              | **Confused, slurred speech, combative, delirious, unconscious** |
| **Core Temperature** | < 40°C (< 104°F)                       | **> 40°C (> 104°F)**                                            |
| **Skin**             | Pale, cool, profuse sweating           | Hot, flushed (may be dry OR covered in sweat)                   |
| **Action**           | Rest in shade, hydrate, active cooling | **Call 911/112, Rapid Immersion Cooling NOW**                   |

The hallmark of **heat stroke** is **central nervous system dysfunction** (confusion, ataxia, irrational behavior, seizures).

### The "Cool First, Transport Second" Mandate

1. **Cold Water Immersion**: The fastest way to pull heat from a boiling human core is full immersion in cold water (stirred ice water bath).
2. **Evaporative Cooling**: If no tub or stream exists, strip clothing, soak the skin with water, and fan vigorously to maximize convective and evaporative heat transfer.
3. **Targeted Ice Packing**: Pack ice bags wrapped in thin cloth directly over high-volume superficial blood vessels: the carotid arteries in the neck, the axillary vessels in the armpits, and the femoral vessels in the groin.
