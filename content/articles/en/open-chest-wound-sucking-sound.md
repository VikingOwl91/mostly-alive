A penetrating injury to the thoracic cage—from a gunshot wound, stabbing, impalement on a fence, or industrial shrapnel—can puncture through the skin, intercostal muscles, and parietal pleura into the pleural cavity.

When the diameter of the chest wall hole reaches approximately **two-thirds the diameter of the trachea**, airflow follows the path of least mechanical resistance: air rushes through the wound rather than through the windpipe on every inhalation.

### The Physics of the Sucking Chest Wound (Open Pneumothorax)

Normally, the human respiratory system functions as a closed negative-pressure bellows:

- During inhalation, the diaphragm descends and the intercostal muscles expand the rib cage, creating negative intrathoracic pressure ($-5\text{ to }-8\text{ cm }H_2O$).
- Atmospheric air rushes down the trachea to inflate the elastic lungs.
- **The Defect**: When the chest wall is pierced, negative intrathoracic pressure sucks ambient air directly through the hole into the pleural space (_open pneumothorax_).
- Blood bubbles and froths around the wound margins with an audible "sucking" or "slurping" sound, while the lung on the injured side collapses under atmospheric pressure.

### The Evolution into a Tension Pneumothorax: The Improvised Seal Danger

In older first aid manuals, laypeople were often taught to tape plastic wrap tightly on all four sides over a chest wound.

In clinical trauma care, **an airtight non-vented seal is potentially lethal**:

- If the lung beneath the wound has an internal laceration, air continues to leak from the lung tissue into the pleural cavity on every breath.
- If the exterior hole is sealed airtight, the air cannot escape. Intrapleural pressure spikes rapidly, transforming a manageable open pneumothorax into a **fatal tension pneumothorax**.
- High-pressure air shifts the heart and mediastinum toward the opposite side, kinking the superior and inferior vena cava. Venous return to the heart drops to zero, causing instantaneous **obstructive cardiac arrest**.

### The Modern First Aid Solution: Vented Seals and "Burping"

1. **Commercial Vented Chest Seal**: Use a modern chest seal equipped with one-way exhaust channels (such as Halo or FoxSeal Vented). The valve vents air and blood outward during exhalation but seals tight during inhalation.
2. **The Layperson Rule (No Seal Available)**: If no commercial vented seal is present, leave the wound uncovered and wipe excess blood, or cover loosely with a non-occlusive clean dressing.
3. **The "Burping" Maneuver**: If a sealed patient deteriorates with worsening breathlessness, blue lips, and rapid pulse, **immediately peel back one side of the seal**. You will often hear a loud hiss of escaping air, which immediately restores cardiac filling.
