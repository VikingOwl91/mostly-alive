A tornado is a violently rotating column of air extending from the base of a cumulonimbus supercell thunderstorm to the surface of the earth.

With rotational wind speeds that can exceed **300 to 450 km/h (200 to 300 mph)**, a tornado generates destructive kinetic energy that turns ordinary construction materials—2x4 wooden studs, roofing tiles, and sheet metal—into supersonic aerodynamic missiles capable of piercing reinforced masonry.

### The Highway Overpass Death Trap

One of the most dangerous, persistent survival myths in storm chasing culture is the belief that driving under a highway overpass and climbing into the concrete girders provides a safe sanctuary.

Atmospheric physics and emergency post-incident analyses have proven this is a **fatal death trap**:

- **The Venturi Effect**: The narrow clearance beneath an elevated highway overpass acts as a geometric constriction channel.
- As the tornado passes over, the constriction forces high-velocity winds through the narrow opening, **accelerating local wind speeds** far beyond ambient levels.
- **The Vortex Sweep**: Occupants perched under the bridge are blasted by accelerated wind and high-density debris, losing their grip and being thrown hundreds of meters into open fields.

### The "Open the Windows" Fallacy

An old myth claimed that homes explode during tornadoes because of a sudden drop in barometric pressure, and that opening windows "equalizes the pressure."

- **Aerodynamic Uplift**: Buildings do not explode from static barometric pressure differences; they fail because of **dynamic wind loads**.
- When strong winds hit an enclosed house, the wind flows over the roof, creating negative pressure (_aerodynamic lift_, like an airplane wing).
- If you open windows on the windward side, high-velocity air enters the building, creating an explosive positive internal pressure that pushes the roof upward while exterior suction pulls from above. The roof detaches instantly, causing total structural collapse.
- **The Protocol**: Keep windows closed. Waste zero seconds on windows. Head directly to the basement or an interior ground-floor room.
