When the human body is subjected to intense physical exertion in high ambient temperatures or high relative humidity, the rate of internal metabolic heat production outpaces the body’s physiological cooling capacity.

Under normal conditions, **evaporative sweating** is the primary heat dissipation mechanism. However, when ambient humidity exceeds 75% or when fluid and sodium loss depletes plasma volume, the thermoregulatory system begins to falter along a predictable continuum: from **heat cramps** to **heat exhaustion**, and ultimately to life-threatening **heat stroke**.

### The Spectrum: Heat Exhaustion vs. Heat Stroke

Understanding where a patient sits on the heat illness spectrum is the most critical clinical decision a first responder makes:

- **Heat Exhaustion**:
  - _Pathophysiology_: Acute cardiovascular strain and dehydration. The body is still actively fighting heat: sweating heavily, skin is pale, cool, and clammy, pulse is elevated, and core temperature remains below **40°C (104°F)**.
  - _Key Marker_: **The brain is intact**. The person is fully oriented, answers questions accurately, and understands what is happening.
- **Heat Stroke (The Medical Emergency)**:
  - _Pathophysiology_: Total failure of thermoregulation. Core temperature spikes above **40°C (104°F)**, initiating widespread systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) and thermal cellular death.
  - _Key Marker_: **Central Nervous System (CNS) Breakdown**. The person becomes disoriented, belligerent, slurs their speech, stumbles, suffers seizures, or collapses into a coma. (Sweating may still be present in exertional heat stroke).

### The Hyponatremia Trap: Why Pure Water Can Kill

When an athlete, hiker, or outdoor worker sweats profusely, they lose not only water, but substantial amounts of **sodium chloride ($\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$)**.

- **The Water Intoxication Cascade**: If the person attempts to rehydrate by chugging gallons of pure, salt-free distilled water, the sudden influx of solute-free water dilutes the extracellular sodium concentration (_hyponatremia_).
- **Brain Swelling**: Osmotic pressure shifts excess water out of the bloodstream and into brain cells, causing acute **Exercise-Associated Hyponatremic Encephalopathy (EAHE)**. The patient develops severe headaches, vomiting, seizures, and non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema—often mistaken for simple dehydration.
- **The Protocol**: Always rehydrate with balanced oral electrolyte solutions, sports drinks, or water paired with salted snacks (pretzels, salted nuts).
