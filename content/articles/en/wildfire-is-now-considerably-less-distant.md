Wildfires (forest fires, brush fires, and wildland-urban interface fires) are dynamic, meteorological-scale combustion events driven by dry fuel biomass, steep topography, and high wind velocities.

During extreme weather conditions (high temperatures, relative humidity < 15%, and strong gusty winds), a wildfire flame front can advance faster than an Olympic sprinter, projecting burning embers (_spot fires_) kilometers ahead of the main fire line.

### The Radiant Heat Threat: Why Running in the Open is Fatal

The leading cause of death during wildfire civilian evacuations is not direct contact with flames, but exposure to **intense infrared radiant heat**:

- **The Radiant Heat Flux**: A dense brush or timber flame front generates radiant heat fluxes exceeding 40 to 80 $\text{kW/m}^2$, generating ambient radiant temperatures above **800°C (1,500°F)**.
- **Thermal Airway Necrosis**: Inhaling air superheated by radiant thermal energy instantly sears mucosal membranes in the pharynx and larynx, causing rapid, fatal laryngospasm and pulmonary edema within seconds.
- **The Shielding Effect**: A solid building or a closed automobile acts as a **critical thermal radiation shield**. Even if smoke fills the area and ambient exterior temperatures are extreme, the physical walls block the direct line-of-sight infrared radiation long enough for the narrow flame front to burn past (typically 10 to 15 minutes).

### Topography: The Uphill Fire Acceleration

A common fatal mistake among hikers and rural residents is attempting to flee uphill away from a fire burning in a valley below.

- **Convective Pre-Heating**: As hot gases and flames rise, they tilt forward toward the uphill slope, directly pre-heating and dehydrating vegetation ahead of the fire.
- **Exponential Speed**: A wildfire burning up a 30-degree slope travels **twice as fast** as a fire on flat ground; on a 40-degree slope, it travels **four times as fast**. Never attempt to outrun a fire uphill. Move across the slope (flank) toward burned areas or wide roadways.

### The Natural Fiber Defense

Synthetic athletic clothing (polyester, nylon, Lycra) is derived from petroleum. When exposed to intense radiant heat, synthetic garments do not burn away into ash: they **melt into boiling liquid plastic that adheres permanently to the skin**, causing horrific third-degree burns.

During any wildfire evacuation, wear exclusively **100% natural, heavyweight fibers**: heavy cotton jeans, denim jackets, wool sweaters, and leather work boots.
