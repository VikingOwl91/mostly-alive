Humans spend substantial resources persuading their hair to achieve impressive volume. Occasionally the atmosphere provides this service free of charge.

There will, in fact, be considerable charge.

If your hair begins standing upright, your skin tingles, or nearby metal objects start buzzing or emitting a faint glow (St. Elmo's fire) during convective storm conditions, the local electrical potential difference has climbed into the hundreds of kilovolts per meter. You are standing directly inside an active upward streamer channel.

### Why This Happens

Prior to a cloud-to-ground lightning strike, a stepped leader propagates downward from the cloud base. In response, high-voltage upward streamers shoot up from elevated and conductive objects on the ground—including you. When the two channels connect, a return stroke carrying between 20,000 and 200,000 amperes traverses that path.

Hair stands up because identical electrostatic charges accumulating along each strand repel one another.

### What To Do Right Now

1. **Move Immediately**: You have seconds before a primary discharge. Move toward fully enclosed structures or vehicles.
2. **The Last-Resort Lightning Crouch**: If shelter is physically unreachable across an open expanse, crouch low on the balls of your feet with your heels pressed together. Tuck your head and cover your ears. Touching heels provides a low-resistance path between your feet so that any ground current passes through your feet rather than through your torso and heart.

### Important Limitation

Hair standing upright is an acute emergency sign, but it is **not a guaranteed early-warning system**. Lightning can strike without noticeable electrostatic sensations. If you hear thunder, you are already within striking distance.
