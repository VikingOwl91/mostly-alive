Humans spend substantial resources persuading their hair to achieve impressive volume. Occasionally, the atmosphere provides this service free of charge.

There will, in fact, be considerable charge.

If your hair starts auditioning for a static-electricity demonstration outdoors during a thunderstorm, your skin begins tingling, or nearby metal objects start buzzing and emitting a faint bluish glow (St. Elmo's fire), the local electrostatic potential gradient has surged past several hundred kilovolts per meter. You are standing directly inside an active upward streamer channel.

### The Physics of Pre-Strike Ionization

Prior to a cloud-to-ground lightning discharge, an invisible stepped leader carrying tens of thousands of volts descends from the storm base in discrete 50-meter jumps. In response to this descending electric field, high-voltage positive upward streamers erupt from elevated and conductive objects on the ground—fences, rocks, lone trees, and human heads.

When a downward stepped leader connects with an upward streamer, the circuit closes: a return stroke carrying between 20,000 and 200,000 amperes of current detonates along that ionized plasma channel at one-third the speed of light. Hair stands upright because identical electrostatic charges accumulating along each hair shaft violently repel one another.

### Why This Is Not a Photo Opportunity

In multiple documented historical tragedies, hikers and tourists paused to snap photos of their buoyant, floating hair atop scenic mountain ridges, smiling at the novelty moments before a fatal multi-strike discharge decimated the group. Standing hair is an acute, red-line physical warning that the air surrounding you is actively breaking down dielectric resistance.

### The Critical Limitation

Hair standing upright is an emergency sign, but it is **not a guaranteed early-warning system**. The vast majority of cloud-to-ground lightning strikes occur without any noticeable electrostatic warning at ground level. If you hear thunder rumble, the storm is close enough to strike you directly.
