The municipal electrical grid has firm opinions regarding physical contact. Most of those opinions are uncompromisingly negative.

When a high-voltage transmission or distribution cable snaps and rests on the ground, the earth surrounding the contact point does not simply absorb the charge. It sets up a radial electric field known as a **voltage gradient** (or potential funnel).

### The Danger of Step Potential

Voltage radiates outward from the touchdown point in concentric circles, decreasing with distance. If you take a standard 70-centimeter walking stride near a fallen 10,000-volt line, your front foot may rest on ground at 6,000 volts while your back foot rests on ground at 4,000 volts.

That **2,000-volt potential difference** drives lethal electrical current up your lead leg, across your pelvis, and down your trailing leg.

### How to Evacuate an Energized Zone

1. **Shuffle Movement**: Keep both feet firmly on the ground, in direct contact with each other. Slide your feet forward in tiny increments of 1 to 2 inches (3 to 5 cm). The soles of your shoes must never leave the earth.
2. **The Bunny Hop**: If terrain is too uneven for a shuffle, press your knees and ankles tightly together and hop away with both feet landing simultaneously.
3. **If Trapped in a Car**: Stay put. The tires insulate the chassis, and you remain at a uniform electrical potential. If fire forces an immediate bailout, jump clear of the vehicle without touching the metal body and ground at the same time, landing with both feet clamped together.
