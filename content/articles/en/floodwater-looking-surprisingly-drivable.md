When encountering a flooded roadway, the water often appears placid, shallow, and entirely traversable by a modern motor vehicle with all-wheel drive.

This is a hydrodynamic optical illusion that accounts for over half of all flash-flood fatalities globally.

### The Physics of Automotive Buoyancy

Vehicles are hollow steel and aluminum shells containing several cubic meters of air. When submerged, Archimedes’ principle dictates that a vehicle experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the water it displaces.

- **15 cm (6 inches) of water**: Reaches the undercarriage of most passenger cars, causing loss of traction, brake failure, and engine stalling if ingested into the air intake.
- **30 cm (12 inches) of water**: Generates enough buoyant lift to float most passenger sedans and compact crossovers. Once tires lose contact with the asphalt, steering and braking inputs exert zero control.
- **60 cm (24 inches) of moving water**: Will float and sweep away heavy SUVs, pickup trucks, and delivery vans.

### Lateral Hydraulic Force

Water flowing at just 10 km/h (6 mph) exerts immense lateral dynamic pressure against the broad side profile of a car. Combined with buoyancy, moving floodwaters easily push a multi-ton vehicle into drainage ditches, rivers, or over bridge railings.

### Invisible Sub-Surface Traps

Water obscures what lies beneath:

- The entire asphalt road surface may have washed away, leaving a 3-meter deep sinkhole.
- Storm sewer manhole covers are routinely popped open by hydraulic pressure, creating invisible suction vortices.
- Fallen electrical distribution wires may be actively energizing the puddle.
