Few everyday situations induce claustrophobia quite like being inside an elevator that suddenly shudders, grinds to a halt, and shuts off its motion between floors.

In reality, modern traction and hydraulic elevators are among the most heavily regulated and redundantly engineered mechanical transportation systems in human history.

### The Physics of Safety: Why Free-Fall is a Movie Myth

Hollywood movies regularly depict severed elevator cables causing cabins to plummet thousands of feet down shafts into the basement.

In modern engineering, **an elevator cannot free-fall**:

1. **Multiple Redundant Steel Cables**: A traction elevator is supported by 4 to 8 independent high-tensile steel cables. A single cable is strong enough to support the entire fully-loaded cab by itself.
2. **The Counterweight Balance**: The elevator car is balanced by a heavy counterweight equal to the car weight plus 40–50% of its rated passenger capacity.
3. **The Mechanical Governor and Safety Wedge Brakes**: Even in the theoretically impossible event that every steel cable snapped simultaneously, a mechanical centrifugal overspeed governor triggers automatically. Heavy steel wedge brakes physically bite into the vertical guide rails, mechanically locking the elevator to the building structure within inches without requiring electrical power.

### The True Danger: The Self-Extraction Guillotine

Over 90% of civilian elevator fatalities occur not from equipment failure, but from **passengers attempting to rescue themselves**.

- **The Scenario**: An elevator stops half a meter above or below a floor landing. The occupants pry open the doors and see an opening to the floor above or below.
- **The Shear Trap**: As a person climbs through the gap, their torso is positioned across the threshold. If a technician releases brake pressure in the motor room, or if the drive motor slips, the car moves. The floor threshold acts as a massive mechanical shear, causing catastrophic crushing trauma.
- **The Shaft Fall**: If climbing out into the upper floor, stepping backward can cause you to plunge down the open, dark elevator shaft beneath the car.
- **The Rule**: **Stay inside the cabin**. Press the emergency call button, communicate with dispatch, and wait for trained firefighters or certified elevator technicians to secure the car before stepping out.
