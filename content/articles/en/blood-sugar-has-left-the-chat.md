The human brain relies almost exclusively on circulating blood glucose as its primary metabolic fuel. Unlike muscle or liver tissue, brain cells cannot store meaningful reserves of glycogen.

When blood glucose plummets below **70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L)**, the central nervous system undergoes acute energetic starvation (_neuroglycopenia_).

### Recognizing the Cascade: Adrenergic Storm to Neuroglycopenia

Severe hypoglycemia triggers a two-phase physiological response:

1. **The Autonomic Adrenergic Surge (The Alarm Phase)**: The body floods the bloodstream with adrenaline and glucagon to stimulate hepatic glycogen breakdown:
   - Profuse cold sweat (_diaphoresis_), intense shaking, pallor, tachycardia, extreme hunger, and anxiety.
2. **Neuroglycopenia (The Brain Starvation Phase)**: When glucose falls further (< 50 mg/dL):
   - Confusion, slurred speech, belligerent or bizarre behavioral changes (often mistaken for acute alcohol intoxication), blurred vision, seizure activity, and rapid progression to diabetic coma.

### The Lethal Fallacy: Liquid Sugar in an Unconscious Mouth

A catastrophic error frequently attempted by well-meaning relatives is attempting to pour sugary drinks, honey, or syrup into the mouth of a drowsy or unconscious diabetic.

- **Airway Mechanics**: An individual with depressed consciousness loses pharyngeal motor tone and the laryngeal protective closure reflex.
- **Asphyxiation**: Any fluid poured into the oral cavity bypasses the esophagus and flows directly into the larynx and lungs. The patient suffocates on the very liquid intended to save them.
- **The Rule**: If the patient cannot sit up, hold a cup, and swallow on command, **nothing enters the mouth**. Roll them into the recovery position and call 911 / 112.

### The Rule of 15: Fast Carbs vs. The Chocolate Trap

When a patient is conscious and able to swallow safely:

- **The Chocolate Trap**: People often reach for chocolate bars or cake. Chocolate contains 30% to 50% fat. High lipid content delays gastric emptying, meaning the sugar is trapped in the stomach for 30 minutes while the brain starves.
- **The Rule of 15**: Administer **15 grams of pure, rapid-acting carbohydrate** without fat (150 mL regular fruit juice, 3–4 glucose tablets, or 4 sugar cubes). Wait 15 minutes for the glucose to cross the blood-brain barrier before reassessing.
