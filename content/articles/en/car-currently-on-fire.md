A modern automobile contains dozens of liters of flammable fuel, synthetic hydraulic fluids, pressurized air conditioning gases, kilograms of combustible polyurethane foam, and hundreds of meters of electrical wiring.

When smoke billows from the dashboard or flames lick out from under the front bumper, a localized electrical short can engulf the entire passenger compartment in under three minutes.

### The Oxygen Rush: Why Opening the Hood is Lethal

When a fire starts in the engine bay (often caused by an oil leak contacting an exhaust manifold or an electrical short circuit), the fire is enclosed under the heavy metal hood.

- **Oxygen Starvation**: As flames consume local oxygen, the fire transitions into a smoldering, oxygen-starved state filled with superheated combustible hydrocarbon vapors.
- **The Backdraft Explosion**: If a driver pops the hood latch and lifts the metal lid, a sudden rush of fresh atmospheric oxygen enters the bay. The fuel-rich vapors ignite instantaneously, projecting an explosive fireball several meters into the driver's face, neck, and upper torso.
- **The Protocol**: Keep the hood sealed. The closed metal lid partially restricts oxygen flow and acts as a mechanical barrier protecting passengers as they evacuate.

### Modern Vehicle Hazards: EV Batteries and Pressurized Shrapnel

Vehicle fires produce mechanical and chemical hazards beyond simple heat:

1. **Pressurized Projectiles**: Modern cars feature gas-charged struts (for hoods and tailgates) and pressurized shock absorbers. When heated, internal pressures spike until the metal tubes explode, firing high-velocity steel pistons through the air like artillery shells.
2. **Electric Vehicle (EV) Thermal Runaway**: A damaged lithium-ion battery pack can experience self-sustaining _thermal runaway_, where cells generate their own oxygen as they burn at over 1,000°C (1,800°F). EV battery fires release dense clouds of **hydrogen fluoride (HF)**, an extremely corrosive gas that destroys lung tissue on contact.
3. **Evacuation Distance**: Evacuate all occupants to at least **30 meters (100 feet)** away, positioned strictly **upwind** of smoke plumes and behind protective crash barriers.
