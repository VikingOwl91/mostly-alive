Crocodilians (crocodiles, alligators, and caimans) are apex ambush predators refined by more than eighty million years of unchanged mechanical efficiency. They do not hunt by chasing prey over hills; they hunt by exploiting the transition zone between liquid and land.

### Phase 1: Water-Edge Spatial Discipline

Over 90% of non-fatal and fatal crocodilian encounters occur within five meters of the water's edge.

- **The Five-Meter Rule**: In known crocodilian habitats (such as northern Australia, the southeastern United States, Central America, and tropical river basins), the shoreline is not a viewing platform. Maintain a minimum setback of five meters (15 to 20 feet) from all banks, boat ramps, and pontoons.
- **Visual Deception**: Murky water conceals 99% of an animal's body mass. A five-meter saltwater crocodile can rest motionless in sixty centimeters of murky water with only its nostrils exposed.
- **Attractant Control**: Never clean fish, discard bait, or wash cooking gear at the water's edge. Crocodilians remember feeding sites and patrol boat ramps systematically.
- **High-Risk Windows**: Avoid water activities between dusk and dawn, when predatory activity peaks and human visual range is at its lowest.

### Phase 2: Terrestrial Encounters and the Zigzag Fallacy

If you encounter an alligator or crocodile on dry land:

1. **The Straight-Line Sprint**: One of the most widespread and dangerous survival myths advises running in a zigzag pattern. This recommendation is completely unsupported by wildlife authorities. Crocodilians have forward-oriented bursts of terrestrial speed (up to 15–30 km/h for brief seconds in a "gallop" or high walk), but their anaerobic physiology means they exhaust their energy reserves within seconds. Running zigzag slows your speed and keeps you within striking reach. Run straight, run fast, and put immediate linear distance between you and the water.
2. **Defensive Hissing**: If an animal on land hisses with an open mouth, it is warning you away from its territory, basking spot, or nest. Back away smoothly while maintaining visual contact.

### Phase 3: Active Attack Defense (Eyes and Palatal Valve)

If physical contact occurs, you are in a life-threatening struggle. **Never play dead.** Crocodilians are opportunistic scavengers; passivity guarantees that you will be dragged into deep water and subjected to the "death roll" (rotational shearing used to dismember prey and induce rapid drowning).

- **The Eyes**: The eyes are the softest and most vulnerable target on an otherwise armored skull. Drive your thumbs, fingers, or sharp objects directly into the eye sockets with maximum force.
- **The Palatal Valve**: Behind the tongue lies a fleshy membrane (the palatal valve) that seals the throat from incoming water while the mouth is open. If dragged into the water, shoving your hand, arm, or a branch down the throat into this valve allows water to rush into the trachea. The animal will release its grip in an involuntary reflex to prevent drowning.
- **The Snout**: Hard, repetitive blows with heavy rocks or sticks to the sensitive sensory pits on the tip of the snout can induce release.

### Phase 4: Post-Attack Trauma and Critical Infection

A survived crocodilian attack requires immediate emergency medical intervention, even if puncture wounds initially appear manageable.

- **Hemorrhage Control**: Crocodilian jaws exert thousands of pounds per square inch of crush force, frequently severing major arteries and shattering bone. Apply commercial arterial tourniquets high and tight on wounded limbs immediately.
- **Microbiological Crisis**: Crocodilian oral cavities and murky aquatic environments contain hyper-virulent bacterial pathogens, including *Aeromonas hydrophila*, *Vibrio*, *Pseudomonas*, and anaerobic species. Infection sets in rapidly, causing extensive tissue necrosis and sepsis. Immediate hospital debridement and broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotic therapy are mandatory.
