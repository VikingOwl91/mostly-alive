Human skin (the epidermis and dermis) is an organ composed primarily of structural collagen and keratin proteins. When subjected to temperatures exceeding 44°C (111°F), these cellular proteins begin denaturing. At 60°C (140°F)—the typical temperature of commercial hot beverages and unmoderated tap water—irreversible thermal necrosis of the full epidermal layer occurs in under five seconds.

A common physics error during thermal injuries is assuming that damage ceases the moment the heat source is removed. Heat stored in the dermal fluid matrix continues conducting deeper into the capillary beds for several minutes, converting minor superficial injuries into deep partial-thickness burns unless actively extracted.

### The Physics and Biology of 20-Minute Water Cooling

Clinical burn research demonstrates that cooling with **cool or lukewarm running tap water (10°C to 20°C / 50°F to 68°F) for a full 20 minutes** is the single most effective first-aid intervention:
1. It halts heat progression into deeper subcutaneous tissue layers.
2. It stabilizes cellular membrane potential, reducing pain sensation at exposed nociceptors.
3. It limits inflammatory histamine release and microvascular permeability, dramatically reducing post-burn edema and subsequent grafting requirements.

### Why Ice Is Strictly Prohibited

Applying ice or freezing water to burned skin triggers an acute biological disaster. The extreme cold causes intense local vasoconstriction of the remaining capillary beds, depriving the compromised dermal zone of oxygen and nutrients. Furthermore, frozen ice crystals inflict direct frostbite mechanical damage onto thermally fragile cells, transforming a survivable burn into dead, necrotic tissue.

### Dressing and Blister Mechanics

- **Cling Film (Plastic Wrap)**: Clean kitchen cling film is the ideal primary burn dressing. It is sterile on the roll, non-adherent (preventing the agony of gauze adhering to raw dermis), transparent (allowing medical staff to inspect the wound without removing dressings), and seals in moisture. Always apply it loosely in flat sheets—never wrap it circumferentially around a limb.
- **Blisters**: The fluid dome of a burn blister (containing sterile plasma, fibrin, and antimicrobial peptides) provides a perfect protective biological barrier. Puncturing blisters exposes the raw dermis directly to bacterial colonization, drastically increasing the risk of invasive cellulitis and permanent scarring.
