When a storm, vehicle collision, or falling tree downs a high-voltage power transmission line directly onto an automobile, the vehicle becomes energized at thousands of volts.

To bystanders and frightened passengers, the scene looks terrifying—tires may smoke, sparks may shower, and electrical hums reverberate through the chassis.

Yet the physics of electrical potential creates a surprising paradox: **the inside of the car is the safest place you can possibly be.**

### The Faraday Cage and the Equipotential Bubble

Electricity does not shock an organism merely because voltage is present; current flows only when there is a **difference in electrical potential** ($V = I \cdot R$) between two points in contact with the body.

- **The Metal Frame**: The conductive steel and aluminum body of an automobile forms an _equipotential shell_ (a partial Faraday cage). High-voltage current flows around the exterior metal skin and arcs through the rubber tires to the ground.
- **Inside the Cabin**: As long as you remain completely inside without reaching out, your entire body is at the exact same electrical potential as the vehicle. No electrical current passes through your organs.
- **The True Killer**: The moment an occupant attempts to step out normally—placing one shoe on the wet earth while their hand is gripping the metal door frame—they establish a direct short-circuit between the energized 10,000-volt car and the zero-volt ground. Current surges through the chest, inducing instant fatal ventricular fibrillation.

### The Only Exception: The Emergency Jump-Clear Protocol

You must remain inside the vehicle until the power company physically confirms the line is de-energized.

The **only reason** to leave the car before the line is cut is if the vehicle catches fire and imminent smoke or flames threaten your life:

1. **Do Not Touch and Step**: Do not climb down. Open the door fully by pushing the plastic interior handle.
2. **The Clean Leap**: Stand on the door sill. Keep your arms tucked tightly against your chest. **Jump completely clear of the vehicle**, landing on both feet simultaneously on the ground. At no microsecond during the jump may your body touch the car and the ground at the same time.
3. **The 10-Meter Shuffle**: Once on the ground, high-voltage electricity radiating from the car creates concentric rings of voltage (_step potential_). Walking with normal strides creates a deadly voltage difference between your left and right feet. Keep your feet touching side-by-side and **shuffle with tiny, sliding steps** without lifting your soles until you are at least 10 meters (33 feet) away.
