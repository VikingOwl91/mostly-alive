In colloquial language, "shock" often refers to emotional distress or psychological surprise after hearing bad news.

In clinical medicine, **circulatory shock** is an acute, life-threatening cardiovascular breakdown where systemic microcirculatory blood flow falls so low that oxygen delivery fails to meet cellular metabolic demands (_tissue hypoperfusion_).

Without immediate intervention, cellular anaerobic metabolism leads to massive lactic acidosis, multi-organ failure, and irreversible death.

### The Mechanisms: Why the Skin Turns Cold and Clammy

Circulatory shock can result from massive blood loss (_hypovolemic shock_), pump failure (_cardiogenic shock_), severe sepsis or anaphylaxis (_distributive shock_), or pulmonary embolism (_obstructive shock_).

When systemic blood pressure drops:

1. **Centralization (Sympathetic Vasoconstriction)**: The autonomic nervous system clamps down peripheral arterioles in the skin, muscles, and abdominal organs to divert remaining oxygenated blood to the brain and heart.
2. **The Clinical Signature**: Skin turns ghostly pale, cold, and clammy with sweat; the radial pulse becomes rapid and thready (> 100 bpm); breathing becomes rapid and shallow (_tachypnea_); and capillary refill time exceeds 2 seconds.

### The Lethal Triad of Trauma

In hemorrhagic shock, hypothermia is an active biological killer:

- **The Lethal Triad**: Hypothermia ($< 35^\circ\text{C}$), metabolic acidosis (pH $< 7.2$), and coagulopathy (clotting failure).
- When a trauma patient loses blood and is exposed to the ambient cold ground, their body temperature drops rapidly.
- At core temperatures below 35°C, the coagulation factor enzyme cascade loses more than 50% of its function. Blood will no longer clot, accelerating internal hemorrhage regardless of how many platelets are present.
- Wrapping a shock patient in **thermal blankets and space foil** is a critical surgical life-support intervention.

### The Passive Leg Raise (Autotransfusion)

If the patient has no head injury, spinal fracture, or shortness of breath:

- Laying the patient flat on their back and elevating the legs 20 to 30 cm shifts approximately **300 to 500 mL of venous blood** from the capacious leg veins back into the inferior vena cava and right heart chambers.
- This transiently boosts cardiac output and stabilizes cerebral perfusion while awaiting advanced emergency medical services.
