An earthquake occurs when tectonic friction along a geological fault line suddenly slips, releasing decades of accumulated elastic strain in the form of destructive high-frequency seismic body waves (P-waves and S-waves) and rolling surface waves (Love and Rayleigh waves).

In the opening seconds of a major seismic event, the earth does not simply vibrate: the ground heaves violently in multidirectional horizontal and vertical accelerations ($g\text{-force}$ exceeding 1.0 g).

### The Doorway Myth and the "Triangle of Life" Fallacy

Over the past fifty years, outdated folklore has led people to adopt dangerous behaviors during earthquakes:

1. **The Doorway Myth**: In 19th-century unreinforced adobe brick houses, the doorway was sometimes the only wooden structural arch remaining after walls collapsed. In modern architecture, doorways are standard partition frames that offer zero additional structural load capacity. Standing in a doorway leaves you completely exposed to falling ceiling fixtures, while the violently swinging door acts as a heavy wooden club striking your skull.
2. **The "Triangle of Life" Myth**: An internet chain letter claimed that occupants should curl into a fetal position beside large furniture rather than underneath it. Extensive structural analysis by FEMA and the Red Cross proved that falling ceiling tiles, lighting fixtures, and wall plaster strike people lying in the open beside objects. **Being underneath a sturdy table (_Drop, Cover, Hold On_) is statistically proven to prevent over 85% of non-fatal and fatal earthquake injuries.**

### The Exterior Façade Kill Zone

When ground shaking begins, the overwhelming instinctive impulse of untrained people is to sprint toward the exit.

- **The Exterior Envelope**: The exterior perimeter of a modern urban building is the most hazardous location on earth during an earthquake.
- **Projectiles**: As the building flexes, thousands of square meters of tempered glass windows shatter, heavy decorative masonry parapets detach, and air conditioning units plunge from roofs.
- **The Protocol**: **Stay inside**. Drop under a heavy desk or against an interior wall, hold on, and wait until shaking stops completely before exiting carefully via stairwells.
