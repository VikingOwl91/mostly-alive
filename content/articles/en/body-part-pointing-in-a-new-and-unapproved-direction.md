A high-velocity sports impact, fall from height, or vehicular crash can exceed the mechanical tensile strength of human bone (causing a **fracture**) or tear the stabilizing fibrous capsule of an articulating joint (causing a **dislocation**).

The immediate visual presentation is often alarming: an arm or leg bent at an unnatural angle, an abnormal bulge at the shoulder, or a bone fragment protruding through the dermis.

### The Movie Trope: Why "Popping It Back In" is Dangerous

Hollywood action movies regularly portray heroes clenching their teeth and violently slamming a dislocated shoulder back into its socket against a tree or wall.

In real human anatomy, **amateur joint reduction is an orthopaedic disaster**:

- Surrounding every major joint is a dense neurovascular bundle (_e.g., the axillary nerve and brachial artery at the shoulder; the popliteal artery and tibial nerve at the knee_).
- When a joint dislocates, these vessels are stretched taut like guitar strings over sharp bony edges.
- Forcible manipulation without muscle relaxation and fluoroscopic guidance regularly tears vessel walls, lacerates nerves, or traps soft tissues, turning a simple dislocation into irreversible limb ischemia and permanent paralysis.

### The pDMS Neurovascular Examination

Whenever a bone or joint is injured, first responders perform an immediate **CSM (Circulation, Sensation, Motor)** check distal to the injury:

1. **Circulation**: Feel for the peripheral pulse (radial pulse for arms, foot pulse for legs) and observe capillary refill time (< 2 seconds).
2. **Sensation**: Lightly touch the patient's fingers or toes. Ask if they feel normal sensation or tingling/numbness.
3. **Motor**: Ask the patient to gently wiggle their digits without moving the proximal fracture site.

If a distal pulse is absent, the deformed bone or displaced joint is kinking or compressing an arterial vessel. This is a red-alert emergency requiring rapid paramedic intervention.

### The Rule of Open Fractures

If a fractured bone has torn through the skin (_open fracture_), the primary goal is infection prevention:

- Exposed bone is porous and absorbs dirt, bacteria, and clothing debris.
- **Never push the bone back inside**. Cover the bone loosely with a sterile dressing, immobilize the entire limb with soft padding, and keep the patient fasting for urgent surgical debridement and internal fixation.
