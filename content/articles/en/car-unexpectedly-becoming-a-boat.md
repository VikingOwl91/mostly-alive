Automobiles are designed with impressive hydraulic seals, but they are not submarines. When a vehicle plunges into deep water, it does not sink instantaneously like a stone; it floats for between 30 and 120 seconds.

This floating window is your only predictable opportunity for self-rescue.

### The Lethal Myth of Pressure Equalization

Popular folklore suggests waiting calmly inside the car until the cabin completely fills with water, at which point the pressure inside and outside equalizes, allowing you to open the door.

In reality, waiting for equalization means:

- You are submerged in dark, freezing, disorienting water.
- You have depleted your oxygen supply during high-stress hyperventilation.
- Debris, luggage, and floating child seats block your exit.
- The car has rolled upside down due to engine weight.

### The Verified SWOC Protocol

Developed by cold-water physiologist Dr. Gordon Giesbrecht through hundreds of live submersion trials, the **SWOC** protocol must be executed without hesitation:

1. **S — Seatbelts**: Unbuckle immediately. If children are present, unbuckle yourself first, then reach back to release children.
2. **W — Windows**: Open both front side windows immediately. Modern electric power windows function for at least 30 to 60 seconds after water entry before circuits short.
3. **O — Out**: Exit directly through the window opening.
4. **C — Children First**: Push older children out the window first, then hand infants out to them or hold them above your head as you swim out.

### If Electrical Windows Fail

Strike the bottom corner of a **side window** using a hardened metal center-punch or emergency window hammer. Tempered glass will instantly shatter into thousands of dull cubes. Never attempt to shatter the windshield, which is laminated with a polyvinyl butyral interlayer and cannot be kicked or punched out underwater.
