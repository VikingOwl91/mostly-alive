Cooking oil is an agreeable culinary lubricant until it crosses its autoignition temperature (typically 350°C to 400°C / 660°F to 750°F), at which point it decides to burn continuously and enthusiastically.

Your instinct when seeing flames is often to grab the nearest glass of water. Suppress this instinct with extreme prejudice.

### The Physics of the Catastrophe

Water is denser than oil and has a boiling point of 100°C (212°F). Hot burning oil sits at ~380°C.

When you pour liquid water into burning oil, the water plunges directly beneath the oil surface and instantly flash-boils into steam. A single drop of water expands approximately **1,700 times** its volume as steam. This violent phase change blasts millions of microscopic droplets of boiling oil upward into the surrounding air, creating a massive fuel-air aerosol. The aerosol catches fire simultaneously, creating a 3-meter fireball that engulfs the ceiling, cabinets, and whatever human was holding the glass.

### Correct Suppression Protocol

1. **Cover with Metal**: Slide a snug metal lid, baking sheet, or certified fire blanket over the top of the pot. Sliding it from the front shields your hands from flames.
2. **Cut the Energy**: Turn off the stove burner.
3. **Do Not Move the Pan**: Moving an open burning pan sloshes 350°C burning liquid onto floors, walls, and clothing.
4. **Wait for Cooling**: Do not lift the lid to peek. As long as the oil remains above its autoignition temperature, introducing fresh air will trigger instant re-ignition.
