### The Source Supremacy Principle

Mostly Alive operates under a strict **Source Supremacy Protocol**:

1. **Hypothesis vs. Requirement**: All preliminary notes and common survival tips are treated strictly as research hypotheses to be tested empirically against current peer-reviewed or statutory guidance.
2. **The Source Wins**: If an authoritative primary standard contradicts our initial phrasing or popular assumption, the primary source overrides without exception.
3. **Anti-Confirmation Bias**: Research queries target official guideline documents (e.g., ERC Resuscitation Guidelines, NFPA codes, NOAA meteorology protocols), not search results curated to confirm pre-existing text.
4. **Jurisdiction & Version Tracking**: Where clinical or statutory procedures diverge geographically (e.g., EU 112 vs US 911 dispatch, varying resuscitation standards), national variations and specific guideline editions are explicitly documented.

### Myth Busting & Scientific Verification

Many widespread survival tips are lethal folklore:

- **Myth**: _"Wait for the sinking car to fill completely with water to equalize pressure before opening the door."_  
  **Reality**: Fatal in most trials. The verified SWOC protocol (Seatbelts off, Windows open, Out, Children first) mandates window egress within the initial 60-second floating phase.
- **Myth**: _"Rub snow or massage cold limbs to rewarm a hypothermic person."_  
  **Reality**: Triggers immediate afterdrop and lethal cardiac arrhythmias by forcing acidotic peripheral blood into the heart. Rewarming must focus on the torso.
- **Myth**: _"Shelter under beech trees during lightning storms."_  
  **Reality**: All isolated tall trees create severe side-flash arcs, ground step potential, and explosive steam shrapnel regardless of botanical species.

### Provenance Classification

- `reviewed`: 100% of actionable claims are verified against cited primary institutional standards, with named reviewers and recorded audit dates.
- `draft` / `needs-review`: Content under active verification. Displayed with prominent cautionary banners in the user interface.
